Wrong glossary terms in streaming speech translation: no more than offline

Posted on 2026-11-18
I gave a speech LLM a glossary with one wrong entry and translated the same 116 English sentences into Chinese twice: offline on the full audio, and simultaneously on growing 1-second prefixes with local agreement. The wrong term ended up in 11 outputs offline and 12 streaming; the 95% interval of the difference stays below 6% of the items.

A speech translation system for talks is often given a glossary: a list of the talk’s terms with the Chinese form the speaker or the organiser wants. Glossaries are written by people and some entries are wrong. A model that follows the glossary will then write the wrong term. In simultaneous speech translation the model also has to commit to text before the sentence is over, while it has heard only part of the audio. My worry was that a model working from a half-heard sentence leans harder on the glossary, and so copies a wrong entry more often than when it hears the whole sentence.

I measured it on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, translating English talks from ACL 60/60 into Chinese. The short answer is no. Of 116 sentences, the wrong glossary term ended up in the final translation of 11 offline and 12 streaming. I had fixed the bar in advance at 10% of the items (11.6 sentences); the 95% interval of the difference reaches at most 5.2%. The streaming decoder does put the wrong term into its intermediate hypotheses more often (31 sentences), but most of those are dropped before they are committed, and none is committed before the speaker has said the term.

What I measured

Sentences and terms. ACL 60/60 has English talks from ACL 2022 with reference Chinese translations in which technical terms are marked. I took 116 sentences from its dev and eval sets, from 10 talks, each containing one term (for example tagger, whose reference form is 标记器). Each sentence is one item.

Four glossary conditions. The glossary for an item is the list of its talk’s terms, 7 to 20 entries (median 13), one line per term in the form tagger → 标记器. Only the line of the item’s term changes between conditions:

Condition The term’s line What it shows
none no glossary at all the model on its own
correct tagger → 标记器 whether the model uses the glossary
wrong tagger → 分类器 how often the model copies a wrong form
irrelevant the line is replaced by an entry for a term that is not in the sentence how often the wrong form appears with nothing pointing to it

The wrong form is the correct Chinese for a different term of the same kind from another talk (分类器 means classifier). It is never an acceptable translation of the spoken term. The prompt puts the audio first, then the glossary, then the instruction:

What I count. An item counts as an insertion if its final translation contains the wrong form under the wrong glossary. From that I subtract the items whose translation contains the same form under the irrelevant glossary, where nothing in the prompt suggests it. The result is the insertion count: the number of sentences in which the wrong entry put the wrong term into the translation. Han forms are matched as substrings, Latin forms on word boundaries. Each match was then read in its context, with the condition hidden, and judged as either the model’s rendering of the spoken term or something else (another sense, part of a longer word, a copy of the glossary list). All 23 matches below (11 offline, 12 streaming) were judged renderings of the term.

Two ways to decode, same model and prompt. Greedy decoding, at most 256 new tokens, one RTX 4090.

  • Offline: the model gets the whole sentence’s audio and translates once. The segments are 2.1 to 22 s long, median 7.4 s.
  • Streaming: the model gets the first 1.0 s of audio, then the first 2.0 s, and so on, until the full segment. The output is built with local agreement over two consecutive steps (LA-2; Liu, Spanakis and Niehues 2020, used for simultaneous speech translation by Polák et al. 2022). At each step, the text committed so far is forced as the start of the model’s answer and the model continues from it. The longest common prefix of the last two steps’ continuations is committed and can no longer change. At the last step, with the full audio, the whole continuation is committed.

The prompt, the glossary and the weights are the same in both. The only difference is whether the model sees the whole audio at once or growing prefixes of it.

How simultaneous is the streaming output? I measured latency on the audio clock (compute time not included), with each Chinese character timed at the step that committed it. Under the wrong glossary, LAAL (Papi et al. 2022) is 2.4 s and AL (Ma et al. 2019) is 2.2 s, on sentences whose median length is 7.4 s.

Why the baseline is low to begin with

The speech LLMs I tested were already reluctant to copy a wrong glossary entry offline. The same prompt and the same 116 items, with the counts on the same definition:

Model Input Insertion count, of 116
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (text-only LLM) gold English transcript 59
Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct gold English transcript 4
Qwen2.5-Omni-7B gold English transcript 7
Qwen2.5-Omni-7B audio, offline 11

A text-only LLM given the transcript writes the wrong term in about half the sentences. The speech LLMs do it in a handful, from text and from audio alike. Qwen2-Audio uses correct glossary entries and declines wrong ones even when told to use the glossary. So the question for streaming is whether partial audio pushes a speech LLM from the low row towards the high one.

The model does read the glossary while streaming: in the correct condition, 10 of 10 sampled streaming outputs used the glossary’s form for the term.

Streaming against offline

Offline Streaming (LA-2, 1.0 s steps)
Wrong form in the output, wrong glossary 11 12
Wrong form in the output, irrelevant glossary 0 0
Insertion count 11 12

The difference is +1 sentence. Three sentences have the wrong term only in the streaming output, two only in the offline output; the other nine have it in both.

To get an interval I resampled the 10 talks with replacement 9,999 times, because sentences from one talk share a speaker and a glossary. The 95% interval of the difference, as a share of the 116 items, is −0.035 to 0.052. Its upper end, about 6 sentences, is below the 10% bar (11.6 sentences) I set before decoding. A rise of a few sentences is not excluded; a rise of 10% of the items is.

The five sentences that differ are worth reading, because they are not one effect:

Sentence Term → correct / wrong form Offline Streaming
dev 119 oracle → 甲骨文 / 可解释性 可解释的 (“interpretable”) 可解释性 (“interpretability”)
dev 147 tagger → 标记器 / 分类器 分词器 (“tokenizer”) a sentence assembled from the glossary, below
eval 185 VALSE → VALSE / MAWPS VALSE VALSE（MAWPS）
dev 44 OOV → OOV / API API OOV
eval 251 extractor → 提取器 / 解码器 文本解码器 (“text decoder”) 提取器

In dev 119 both outputs follow the wrong entry; the offline one changed one character and so escaped the string match. Eval 185 is a real streaming insertion: the model kept the correct name and attached the wrong one in brackets. Dev 44 and eval 251 go the other way: offline copied the wrong entry and streaming did not. Dev 147 is the interesting one.

A sentence written from the glossary

Dev 147 is “The part of speech tagger uses a bidi bidirectional inference which improves upon the more often used Viterbi algorithm for decoding.” (10.6 s). In the forced alignment of the transcript, tagger ends at 1.5 s and bidirectional at 3.26 s. Here is what the streaming decoder did under the wrong glossary:

Audio heard Hypothesis at this step Committed after this step
1.0 s: “The part of speech” 演讲的语法部分 (“the grammar part of the speech”) nothing
2.0 s: “… tagger uses a” KinyaBERT 使用了 BERT 的标记化和 BPE，同时实现了无监督的多语言句法分类器。 (“KinyaBERT uses BERT’s tokenization and BPE, and implements an unsupervised multilingual syntactic classifier.”) nothing
3.0 s: “… bidi” the same sentence the whole sentence except the final 。
4.0 and 5.0 s the same sentence no change
6.0 s: “… inference which improves upon” the same sentence + ，通过双向推理提高了分类器的性能 (“, improving the classifier with bidirectional inference”) no change
7.0 to 10.0 s the same sentence as at 2.0 s no change
10.6 s, full audio the same sentence the final 。

At 2.0 s the model had heard seven words. It wrote a complete sentence out of glossary entries: KinyaBERT, BERT, tokenization (标记化), BPE, unsupervised (无监督), multilingual (多语言), syntactic (句法) and the wrong entry for tagger (分类器). “Syntactic classifier” can be read as the model’s rendering of “part of speech tagger” with the glossary’s wrong form, which is how the match was judged; the rest of the sentence is not in the audio. At 3.0 s the model produced the same sentence again, so LA-2 committed it. From then on the committed text could not change. At 6.0 s the model tried to add the bidirectional inference, but the next step dropped it again, so the two steps never agreed and it was never committed. The Viterbi algorithm, said between 7.8 and 9.1 s, never appears.

Offline, under the same wrong glossary, the model translated the sentence (“分词器使用双向推理，这在解码时比更常用的 维特比算法有所改进”, with tokenizer for tagger) and did not use 分类器. Under the irrelevant glossary the streaming decoder started the same way: at 2.0 s it wrote a long paragraph about KinyaBERT. But there the next step did not repeat it word for word, so LA-2 committed only “KinyaBERT 使用了 BERT 的标记化” at 3.0 s. That opening stayed, but the later steps did bring in the audio: bidirectional inference (双向推理) was committed at 5.0 and 6.0 s, and the last step added “用于解码” (for decoding). The Viterbi algorithm reached the hypotheses at 9.0 and 10.0 s and was not committed.

So local agreement is a filter only against hypotheses that change from step to step. A hallucination the model repeats twice gets committed. If dev 147 is counted as a glossary copy rather than an insertion, the difference between streaming and offline is 0 instead of +1.

Wrong forms that appear and are dropped

Each streaming step writes a full hypothesis, of which only the agreed prefix is committed. Counting the wrong form in any hypothesis, committed or not:

Wrong glossary (its wrong form) Irrelevant glossary (its unrelated entry’s form)
Items where the form appears in some hypothesis 31 23
Items where it is in the final output 12 1
… committed before the spoken term ends 0 of 12 0 of 1

The wrong form showed up in a hypothesis of 31 sentences and stayed in 12; in the other 19 it was only in uncommitted text that a later step replaced. The irrelevant glossary’s form, for a term nobody said, showed up in 23 and stayed in 1, so a glossary entry leaking into a partial hypothesis is common whether or not the term is spoken. None of the 12 wrong forms was committed before the alignment says the speaker finished saying the term. Wrong commitments made in anticipation of the speaker were not a source of insertions here.

Holding back the full stop

One design choice in the streaming policy matters more than it looks. The committed text is forced as the start of the model’s answer. If the commit ends with a sentence-final mark such as 。, the model ends its answer right there at every later step, however much audio is left.

I therefore hold back a trailing sentence-final mark or closing quote: while the committed text would end in one, it is shortened by whole tokens, and the mark is committed later together with the text that follows it, or at the last step. Over the 696 streaming outputs (116 items, four conditions, the no-glossary condition decoded three times):

Policy Outputs that stop with 3 s or more of audio left
plain LA-2 36 of 696
LA-2 with the sentence-final mark held back 1 of 696

In all 36 stops under plain LA-2, the committed text ended in 。 and every later step generated nothing. The one remaining stop is dev 147 above: the model kept repeating its glossary sentence, whose 。 was held back, and added nothing.

Scope

Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, English to Chinese on 116 sentences from 10 ACL 60/60 talks, one wrong glossary entry per sentence, greedy decoding, LA-2 on 1.0 s steps of audio. At this size, a true rise of about 15 sentences would be detected in about four of five repetitions of the experiment; a rise of 5 almost never.
